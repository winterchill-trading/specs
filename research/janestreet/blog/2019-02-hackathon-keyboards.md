# Machining the ultimate hackathon prize

- Source: <https://blog.janestreet.com/hackathon-keyboards/>
- Published: 2019-02-28
- Kind: blog post

## Summary

- Jane Street sponsored the MakeMIT hackathon and built custom keyboards as the winners'
  prize, in keeping with the event's maker spirit.
- Spec: 68-key layout (a 60% board plus arrow keys), Cherry MX Brown switches chosen for
  being quiet enough for an office, a walnut case and a laser-engraved brass bottom plate.
- Three main parts: the wooden case, an off-the-shelf PCB (from the Tada68, running QMK
  firmware, with diodes and microcontroller included) and a 1.5 mm brass switch plate.
- The plate was drawn in CAD and laser-cut by an outside service; switches snap into it and
  are soldered through-hole to the PCB. Stabilizer holes keep long keys level.
- No ready-made wooden case fit the 68-key layout, so the case was designed in Rhino,
  moved to Fusion 360 for toolpaths, and cut on a desktop CNC router from planed walnut.
- Machining steps: facing passes to calibrate, an adaptive clearing pass chosen to limit
  tool wear and chips, a finer 1/8 inch cleanup pass, the USB port cut with the block on
  its side, and a stepped ball-end pass for a 5-degree typing slope.
- Assembly uses threaded inserts pressed into the case, keeping the part count tiny.
- The logo plate was generated by a small OCaml DSL over signed distance functions: a shape
  is a function from a point to its distance from the edge, union is a pointwise minimum,
  and marching squares turns the field into polylines for CAM software.
